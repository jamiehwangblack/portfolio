Our Little Happiness


Zinc Plate Etching, 2026

This piece was created on a zinc plate through five iterations of etching with added layers of aquatint throughout the course of several weeks. The Korean text on the top and bottom translates to: "May our little happiness shine brightly upon the world".

To begin, I coated my plate with ground and drew my design with an etching needle.

This is the original sketch I made while planning this piece - it serves to memorialize the space that my spouse and I lived in, from the beginning of our life in Chicago to the end of our first year of marriage.

This reference photo depicts the view from our living room. I added a reflection of my wife and I from the inside of our apartment, combining them into a cohesive memory of the happiness we felt in this space.

This is the first print I pulled after etching my plate in a vat of acid. I wanted to create more depth and contrast in value to emphasize the text and the spotlight around the figures. This was achieved using a technique called "aquatint".

These are the notes I made while planning and documenting the aquatint process. I coated the plate with an acid-resistant grain, applied stop-out to protect certain areas, etched the plate in acid, and repeated this process to gradually build up darker tones.

Only 5 editions were printed of the final piece. The light shining from the kitchen envelops our figure in a bright spotlight, visualizing the feeling of being the main characters in the climax of a romantic film. This piece won 3rd place in my first gallery show, "Transfers: The Art of Printmaking" at Union Street Gallery. Creating this piece was a meaningful experience in which I learnt how to transform a sentimental personal experience into a carefully planned composition. For me, this artwork is a compass guiding me towards my own artistic goals and ambitions.
